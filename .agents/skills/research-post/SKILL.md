---
name: research-post
description: Research a blog post, verify factual claims, and retain useful source provenance before drafting or updating content.
---

Read the brief and existing draft. Identify which claims require evidence. Use primary sources for product capabilities, technical behavior, account requirements, and limitations. Check current sources for facts that can change.

Keep a compact working source ledger outside published content when needed: claim, supporting URL, date checked, and uncertainty. Distinguish source-backed facts, user-provided experiences, and your own inferences. If a source is inaccessible, narrow the claim or flag it; do not replace verification with confident wording.

For service comparisons, use consistent criteria. Separate documentation review from hands-on tests, and record test inputs and outcomes only when tests actually ran. Never submit private URLs to a public archiving service as part of routine research.

Place links near material claims in the published Markdown. Include `verified: "YYYY-MM-DD"` in front matter for time-sensitive guides. Avoid copying source prose; summarize it and quote sparingly. Preserve caveats that could change a reader's choice.
