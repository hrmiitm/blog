You follow a link in an old blog post and get a 404. The site is gone. The article you wanted might still be sitting in a web archive.

Someone had to save it while it was there. Sometimes that someone is an automatic crawler. Sometimes it's a reader who pastes a URL into a box.

## Look for a saved copy

Start with the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/). Enter the original URL and choose an available capture date. You might find the article as it looked years ago, including the images and layout. Missing pieces are common, so check the part you actually need.

If there's no useful copy, search the same URL in [archive.today](https://archive.ph/) or [Ghostarchive](https://ghostarchive.org/). They keep their own captures; a page missing from one archive may exist in another. archive.today saves both a text and a graphical copy.

## Save a page search hasn't found

You don't need to wait for Google to discover a page. If you know its URL and the archive can reach it, you can request a capture directly.

Open [Save Page Now](https://web.archive.org/save/), paste the URL, and save the returned archive link. This captures one page. It [doesn't schedule future captures or crawl the entire site](https://archivesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004651732-Using-The-Wayback-Machine).

For a source you're citing in your own writing, [Perma.cc](https://perma.cc/docs) creates an archived record with a stable citation link. You'll need an account; access depends on your institution or plan.

A page absent from search results can still be public. A page behind a login is a different matter: a remote archive doesn't inherit your browser's access.

## Keep a copy you control

For pages that need scrolling or clicking, [ArchiveWeb.page](https://archiveweb.page/) records content as you browse. Keep the exported archive and reopen it with [ReplayWeb.page](https://replayweb.page/).

If you're preserving a whole collection, [Browsertrix](https://webrecorder.net/browsertrix/) offers automated browser-based crawling. [Archive-It](https://archive-it.org/) is the Internet Archive's subscription service for organizations maintaining curated collections.

For one article, start with a single-page capture. Open the saved copy, find the paragraph or image you wanted, and keep its archive link beside the original. An archived login screen won't be much help later.
